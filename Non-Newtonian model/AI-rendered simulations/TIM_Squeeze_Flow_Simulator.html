4 First-Principles Transport Phenomena Derivation Flow

Complete 6-step mathematical chain from Reynolds Transport Theorem to GPU thermal resistance

Step 1 · Deformable CV & RTT Mass Conservation Conservation Slides 16–37

Choose a cylindrical pillbox of fixed interior radius r < R(t) and moving top boundary z = h(t). The plates at z = 0 and z = h(t) are impermeable, so all volume displaced by the descending cold plate (ḣ < 0) must exit radially through the curved surface area 2πr·h(t):

d/dt (π r² h) + 2π r ∫₀ʰ ur(r,z) dz = 0  ⇒  qr(r) = ∫₀ʰ ur(r,z) dz = r(−ḣ) / 2

Global conservation of the incompressible droplet volume V₀ fixes the moving outer radius until die-edge overflow:

V₀ = π R(t)² h(t)  ⇒  R(t) = min( √[V₀ / (π h(t))], Req )

Step 2 · Cauchy Momentum & Lubrication Scaling Welty Ch. 5–9 & Dim. Analysis

At GPU packaging scales (h ~ 50 µm, R ~ 16.1 mm), the geometric aspect ratio is ε = h/R ≈ 3×10⁻³ ≪ 1 and the squeeze Reynolds number is Re_sq = ρ|ḣ|h/μ ~ 10⁻⁷ ≪ 1. Dropping inertia O(Re_sq) and radial normal-stress gradients O(ε²) reduces cylindrical r-momentum to:

0 = −∂p/∂r + ∂τrz/∂z,    ∂p/∂z ≈ 0  ⇒  p = p(r,t)

Integrating vertically with midplane symmetry τ_rz(r, h/2) = 0 gives the universal linear shear-stress distribution valid for every fluid model:

τrz(r,z) = G(r) · (h/2 − z),    where G(r) ≡ −∂p/∂r > 0

Step 3 · Closure 1: Newtonian Fluid (Stefan Law) τ_rz = μ ∂u_r/∂z

Substituting Newton's law of viscosity and integrating with no-slip boundary conditions u_r(0) = u_r(h) = 0 yields a local parabolic Poiseuille velocity profile. Matching q_r(r) = r(−ḣ)/2 gives:

ur(r,z) = [G(r)/(2μ)] z(h − z),    G(r) = 6 μ r (−ḣ) / h³

Integrating p(r) from r to R (with p(R) = p_∞) and integrating 2πr·p(r) over the disk gives the constant-volume Stefan ODE:

F₀ = 3π μ R⁴(−ḣ)/(2h³) = 3μ V₀²(−ḣ)/(2π h⁵)  ⇒  h(t) = h₀ [ 1 + 8π F₀ h₀⁴ t / (3μ V₀²) ]−1/4

Step 4 · Closure 2: Power-Law Fluid (Scott Law) τ_rz = K |γ̇|ⁿ⁻¹ γ̇

For a shear-thinning particle-filled thermal grease (n < 1), high shear near the walls lowers apparent viscosity, blunting the velocity profile:

ur(r,z) = [n/(n+1)] (G/K)1/n [ (h/2)(n+1)/n − |h/2 − z|(n+1)/n ]

Enforcing the kinematic flux constraint q_r(r) = r(−ḣ)/2 and integrating pressure over the expanding footprint R(t) = √(V₀/πh) yields:

F₀ = [2π K / (n+3)] [(2n+1)/n]ⁿ (V₀/π)(n+3)/2 (−ḣ)ⁿ / h(5n+5)/2  ⇒  h(t) ∝ t−2n/(3n+5)

Step 5 · Closure 3: Bingham Viscoplastic Fluid & Stall Covey–Stanmore Model

Where |τ_rz| ≤ τ_y around the midplane |h/2 − z| ≤ z₀(r) = τ_y/G(r), the paste moves as an unyielded pseudo-plug core (Lipscomb–Denn paradox). Integrating across the sheared wall layers and plug core gives the Buckingham–Reiner flux cubic in ξ₀(r) = 2z₀/h = 2τ_y/(hG):

[h³ G(r) / (12 μp)] (1 − 1.5 ξ₀ + 0.5 ξ₀³) = r(−ḣ) / 2

As −ḣ → 0, ξ₀ → 1 everywhere (G → 2τ_y/h, conical pressure profile), arresting flow permanently at the plastic stall thickness h*:

Fstall = 2π τy R³ / (3h)  ⇒  h* = [ 2 τy V₀3/2 / (3√π F₀) ]2/5,    R* = √[V₀ / (π h*)]

Step 6 · 1D Fourier Conduction & Parallel Air-Gap Network Conservation Slides 48–50

When R(t) < R_eq, the wetted central disk A_cov = πR² conducts heat through TIM (k_TIM) in parallel with the dry perimeter A_air = A_die − A_cov conducting through trapped air (k_air = 0.026 W/m·K):

1 / Reff = kTIM Acov / h + kair (Adie − Acov) / h  ⇒  ΔTTIM = Q · Reff

Because k_TIM / k_air ≈ 192, any Bingham stall that leaves R* < R_eq chokes the effective conduction area and spikes junction temperature T_j = T_cold + ΔT_TIM past the 85 °C throttle limit.
